Evidence

Traumatic brain injury

LAST REVIEWED 2026-05-18 · 346 SOURCES · 0 CORRECTIONS

A 30-person veteran prospective observational study and later neurophysiology work drew attention and require side-by-side design-limit explanation.

Confidence: ModerateSource type: Study
Evidence level
notable open label human signal
Sample sizes
The best-known Nature Medicine report involved 30 male special operations veterans.
Design quality
Open-label observational data can generate hypotheses but cannot establish broad efficacy.
Outcomes
Disability, cognition, PTSD symptoms, depression, anxiety, sleep, and quality of life are separate outcomes.
Limitations
Small sample, male veteran population, Mexico treatment setting, concurrent supports, selection effects, and short follow-up.
Safety signals
Screening, cardiac monitoring, magnesium context, psychiatric history, and follow-up.
What is not proven
Ibogaine is not proven as a general TBI treatment.
Best next source
Nature Medicine 2024 magnesium-Ibogaine veteran study and Nature Mental Health 2025 follow-up.

Key human studies

All published human TBI evidence comes from one open-label cohort: 30 male Special Operations veterans treated under the Stanford MISTIC protocol, plus several secondary analyses that reuse the same 30 people. There is no control group and no randomized trial, so the striking effect sizes below cannot be read as proof of benefit. Ibogaine is not proven as a treatment for traumatic brain injury. Cherian et al., 2024 (MISTIC)Nature Medicine Prospective single-arm open-label observational study; no control group, no blinding (NCT04313712) 30 male U.S. Special Operations Forces veterans with predominantly mild TBI, treated in Mexico Disability (WHODAS) improved immediately (effect size d=0.74) and at 1 month (d=2.20). PTSD (d=2.54), depression (d=2.80), and anxiety (d=2.13) also improved at 1 month. No unexpected or serious adverse events reported under cardiac monitoring. 1 month Open-label and uncontrolled, so improvement cannot be attributed to ibogaine with confidence. Magnesium was co-administered specifically to mitigate ibogaine's known fatal-arrhythmia risk. Three co-authors are affiliated with Ambio Life Sciences, the treatment provider (declared conflict of interest).
Geoly et al., 2026iScience Secondary structural-MRI analysis of the same 30-veteran cohort; not an independent sample Same MISTIC cohort (25 scanned post-treatment, 22 at 1 month) Predicted brain age reduced by about 1.3 years at 1 month; cortical thickness increased in 11 regions and subcortical volume in 8. Authors note T1 scans are sensitive to non-structural changes. 1 month Imaging findings from the same uncontrolled cohort; hypothesis-generating only, not evidence of clinical benefit.
Sridhar et al., 2026Biological Psychiatry: CNNI Secondary functional-neuroimaging analysis (ASL + BOLD fMRI) of the same 30-veteran cohort Same MISTIC cohort Regional cerebral blood-flow increases in cortical, limbic, and striatal regions; left insula and left anterior cingulate changes correlated with disability improvement. 1 month Exploratory and uncontrolled; the authors explicitly call for larger controlled studies. Same 30 participants as the primary report — not independent evidence.
Brown et al., 2025Journal of Affective Disorders Secondary association analysis (mystical-experience questionnaire + EEG) of the same 30-veteran cohort Same MISTIC cohort Stronger mystical experience during treatment was associated with larger PTSD reductions immediately and at 1 month. Correlational only. 1 month Association cannot establish cause. Same cohort as the primary report.

Study details verified against PubMed. These figures describe individual studies and are not evidence of general safety or effectiveness. Ibogaine remains Schedule I in the U.S., is not an approved treatment, and carries serious, documented cardiac risks that require qualified medical supervision.
