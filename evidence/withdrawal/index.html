Evidence

Withdrawal

LAST REVIEWED 2026-05-18 · 346 SOURCES · 0 CORRECTIONS

Withdrawal and craving findings appear in OUD studies, observational detoxification reports, and substance-use reviews.

Confidence: ModerateSource type: Study
Evidence level
early human signal by context
Sample sizes
Varies by source; each claim needs substance type, timing, and follow-up window.
Design quality
Acute symptom change is easier to observe than durable relapse prevention.
Outcomes
Withdrawal severity, cravings, time to relapse, treatment retention, and post-acute symptoms must not be merged.
Limitations
Concurrent medications, setting, expectation, follow-up loss, and substance-specific withdrawal risks can change interpretation.
Safety signals
Withdrawal physiology, dehydration, electrolytes, cardiac risk, benzodiazepines, alcohol, stimulants, opioids, and polysubstance use.
What is not proven
Craving reduction or withdrawal relief is not the same as a cure.
Best next source
OUD papers, clinical trial registries, and adverse-event literature.

Key human studies

Opioid withdrawal is the best-studied human application of ibogaine, but the evidence remains low-tier: open-label observational studies and case series. The only randomized placebo-controlled trial tested noribogaine (the metabolite) and found no statistically significant withdrawal benefit — while confirming a dose-dependent cardiac (QTc) signal. Deaths have occurred in this literature. Ibogaine is not proven in any randomized controlled trial to safely or effectively treat opioid withdrawal. Brown & Alper, 2018The American Journal of Drug and Alcohol Abuse Prospective observational study (no control group, no blinding) Adults with DSM-IV opioid dependence (25 male, 5 female) using oxycodone and/or heroin; averaged 3.1 prior treatment episodes SOWS scores fell from 31.0 +/- 11.6 pretreatment to 14.0 +/- 9.8 at ~76.5 hours posttreatment (p < 0.001). At 1-month follow-up, 15 of 30 subjects (50%) reported no opioid use in the prior 30 days; Addiction Severity Index drug-use scores improved at all timepoints, maximal at 1 month. 1, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months First prospective observational drug-use-outcomes study of ibogaine. Single weight-based oral dose. Open-label, uncontrolled, modest sample, self-reported outcomes. No deaths in this cohort, but design cannot isolate ibogaine's effect from expectancy or co-interventions. Epub 2017; appears in vol 44(1) 2018.
Glue et al., 2016Clinical Pharmacology in Drug Development Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled single ascending-dose trial (of noribogaine, ibogaine's metabolite) Patients established on methadone opioid substitution therapy seeking to discontinue, switched to morphine the preceding week Noribogaine showed only a non-statistically-significant trend toward decreased total opioid-withdrawal score, most notable at the mid dose; study design may have confounded time to resumption of substitution therapy. A concentration-dependent QTc increase was seen (0.17 ms/ng/mL), with largest mean effect of ~16, 28, and 42 ms across ascending dose groups. Single-dose study with pharmacokinetic sampling (mean t1/2 ~24-30 h); no long-term follow-up The ONLY randomized placebo-controlled human study in this set, and it is of the METABOLITE noribogaine, not ibogaine. Did NOT demonstrate significant withdrawal benefit. Dose-dependent QTc prolongation is the headline safety finding. Multiple authors affiliated with DemeRx, Inc. (commercial sponsor) and cardiac-analytics/CRO firms (iCardiac) - clear conflict of interest.
Mash et al., 2018Frontiers in Pharmacology Open-label case series with inpatient medical monitoring (no control group) Volunteers seeking detoxification from opioids or cocaine; inpatient setting (St. Kitts) Reported that a single dose diminished opioid withdrawal symptoms and reduced drug cravings on multi-dimensional craving questionnaires, with no significant adverse events at the doses used in this study. One-month follow-up data (where available) suggested craving effects persisted. 1 month (partial, where available) Largest human case series, but uncontrolled and open-label. Lead author Deborah Mash is commercially linked to ibogaine/noribogaine development (DemeRx) - major conflict of interest. The authors' 'no significant adverse events' claim is specific to this single monitored cohort and contrasts with the broader literature's documented QTc events and deaths; it is not generalizable safety evidence.
Malcolm et al., 2018Journal of Psychoactive Drugs Retrospective observational study (clinic chart data, no control group) Participants with opioid use disorder undergoing a week-long ibogaine detoxification protocol at a Tijuana, Mexico clinic At 48 hours post-administration, withdrawal and craving scores were significantly lower than baseline: 78% showed no objective clinical signs of withdrawal (COWS), 79% reported minimal cravings, and 68% reported subjective withdrawal symptoms only in the mild range (SOWS, BSCS). 48 hours post-administration only (acute) Retrospective (MeSH-indexed Retrospective Studies) and uncontrolled; acute (48 h) outcomes only, so no durability data. Two of three authors affiliated with Crossroads Treatment Center (the commercial clinic providing treatment) - conflict of interest. Authors explicitly call for rigorous controlled trials.
Knuijver et al., 2021Addiction Descriptive open-label observational safety study (university medical center) Patients with opioid use disorder on maintenance treatment, converted to morphine-sulphate, who had failed to reach abstinence with standard care Primary focus was safety: mean maximum QTc (Fridericia) prolongation of 95 ms (range 29-146 ms); 50% reached QTc > 500 ms; in 6 of 14, prolongation > 450 ms lasted beyond 24 h. Severe transient ataxia (unable to walk unaided) occurred in ALL patients. No torsades de pointes observed. Withdrawal/psychomimetic effects were mostly tolerable - 11 of 14 did not return to morphine within 24 h. At least 24 hours of intensive monitoring Most rigorous safety characterization in a hospital setting. Single weight-based dose. Foregrounds the cardiac (QTc > 500 ms, bradycardia) and cerebellar (universal severe ataxia) harms. No torsades or deaths in this small monitored cohort, but the QTc findings substantiate the field's mortality concern. Independent academic group (Radboud UMC / NISPA, Netherlands), no obvious commercial COI.
Noller et al., 2018The American Journal of Drug and Alcohol Abuse Longitudinal observational study (no control group) Individuals receiving legal ibogaine treatment for opioid dependence in New Zealand (50% female) Significant acute reduction in SOWS scores immediately after treatment for all participants (n=14, p=0.015). Among completers (n=8), Addiction Severity Index-Lite drug-use composite (p=0.002) and Beck Depression Inventory-II scores (p<0.001) were significantly reduced at 12 months. 12 months Single ibogaine treatment. Small (n=14, only 8 completers), uncontrolled, self-report-based. ONE PATIENT DIED DURING TREATMENT in this cohort - a stark safety signal in a 14-person study. Co-author Yazar-Klosinski is a full-time salaried employee of MAPS - conflict of interest disclosed in the affiliation. Epub 2017; appears in vol 44(1) 2018.
Alper et al., 1999The American Journal on Addictions Case series, open-label, treatments in non-medical settings (retrospective summary) Heroin-dependent individuals (primarily intravenous use) treated for opioid detoxification Resolution of opioid withdrawal signs without further drug-seeking within 24 hours in 25 of 33 patients, sustained through 72 hours of observation. Other outcomes: drug-seeking without withdrawal signs (4), abstinence with attenuated withdrawal signs (2), drug-seeking with continued withdrawal signs (1), and one fatality possibly involving surreptitious heroin use. 72 hours of post-treatment observation Foundational early case series, but treatments occurred in unsupervised non-medical settings and data were summarized retrospectively. Includes ONE FATALITY (possible covert heroin co-use), an early safety signal. Co-author H.S. Lotsof was the ibogaine anti-addiction patent holder/advocate - conflict of interest. Authors explicitly call for systematic clinical investigation. Indexed as Clinical Trial / Journal Article in PubMed.
Acimovic et al., 2021Forensic Science, Medicine, and Pathology Single forensic case report (autopsy) A heroin-addicted man who self-administered online-purchased iboga (Tabernanthe iboga) extract for detoxification Sudden death 5-12 hours after oral ingestion of iboga powder used for detoxification; autopsy showed organ congestion and pulmonary edema, and toxicology found ibogaine plus morphine and codeine. Death attributed probably to ibogaine's cardiovascular effects. Not applicable (post-mortem) Harm/safety case report, not an efficacy study. Illustrates the lethal cardiac risk of unsupervised, internet-sourced ibogaine used for opioid detoxification, compounded by concurrent opioid use. Included to foreground mortality, not benefit.

Study details verified against PubMed. These figures describe individual studies and are not evidence of general safety or effectiveness. Ibogaine remains Schedule I in the U.S., is not an approved treatment, and carries serious, documented cardiac risks that require qualified medical supervision.
