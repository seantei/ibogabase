Multiple sclerosis (MS)

LAST REVIEWED 2026-09-22 · CASE REPORT ONLY · COI AWARENESS

Ibogaine is not an established MS treatment. At best, public discourse points to case-report–level material. Clinic-adjacent authorship requires conflict-of-interest labeling.

Case report only COI awareness Not proven

Is ibogaine an MS treatment?

Not established. Case-report level at best. Remyelination marketing in media is not an evidence upgrade.

Evidence level
Case report / anecdote density — not confirmatory trials
Conflicts of interest
Clinic-linked reports in the public catalog must be read with COI awareness; commercial proximity does not equal fraud, but it does affect how claims are weighed
What is not proven
Ibogaine as a general MS disease-modifying or curative therapy
Safety
Cardiac / QTc literacy still applies
Self-treatment
DIY refusal

What should readers know about COI in clinic-linked reports?

Ask who funded the work, who employed the authors, whether outcomes were independently adjudicated, and whether adverse events are reported with denominators. See Clinic claims and adverse events and clinic success-rate claim check.

Where is stronger evidence denser (and still limited)?

Compare to OUD and TBI pages — still limited and often observational, but denser than MS on this site.

FAQ

Can I use ibogaine for MS at home?

IbogaBase will not help with self-treatment. See the refusal page.

Does a single case report prove benefit?

No. Case reports generate questions; they do not establish efficacy.
