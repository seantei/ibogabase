Evidence · condition

Multiple sclerosis (MS)

LAST REVIEWED 2026-09-22 · CASE REPORT / NARRATIVE · COI AWARENESS · NOT PROVEN

Ibogaine is not an established MS disease-modifying or curative therapy. Public literature includes Ambio-affiliated case neuroimaging reports and a 2026 narrative review discussing remyelination hypotheses — still far from confirmatory evidence.

Case report / narrative Not proven COI awareness

Is ibogaine an MS treatment?

Not established. Remyelination marketing in media is not an evidence upgrade.

Evidence level
Case report + narrative review — not confirmatory trials
Conflicts of interest
Clinic-linked authorship must be labeled; commercial proximity affects claim weight
What is not proven
Ibogaine as a general MS disease-modifying or curative therapy
Safety
Cardiac / QTc literacy still applies
Self-treatment
DIY refusal

What exists (source-labeled)

Case report with neuroimaging (COI: Ambio-funded / clinic-affiliated)

Chen, Inzunza Domínguez, Valle Uzeta, Pushparaj, Dickinson, 2025. “Case report: Significant lesion reduction and neural structural changes following ibogaine treatments for multiple sclerosis.” Frontiers in Immunology. PMID 39981248, DOI 10.3389/fimmu.2025.1535782. PubMed COI statement: authors affiliated with / shareholders of Ambio Life Sciences; study funded by Ambio with funder involvement in design, data collection/analysis, decision to publish, and manuscript preparation. Label: n-of-few case report + major COI.

Narrative / hypothesis review (not a trial)

Calvey et al., 2026. “Neurorestorative properties of ibogaine: linking multi-receptor affinities to remyelination and metabolic restoration.” Acta Neuropsychiatrica. PMID 41679899, DOI 10.1017/neu.2026.10059. Discusses observational linkages and putative remyelination/metabolic mechanisms; does not establish MS efficacy. Author COI disclosures include psychedelic-industry consulting for some co-authors (see PubMed COI statement).

What does not exist

  • Adequately powered controlled trials of ibogaine for MS with independent adjudication.
  • Regulatory approval of ibogaine as an MS therapy.
  • Any basis for “cures MS” / miracle remyelination language in IbogaBase voice.

How to weigh clinic-adjacent MS claims

Ask who funded the work, who employed the authors, whether imaging/outcomes were independently adjudicated, and whether adverse events are reported with denominators. See Clinic claims and adverse events and clinic success-rate claim check. Compare density to OUD and TBI — still limited, but denser than MS on this site.

FAQ

Can I use ibogaine for MS at home?

IbogaBase will not help with self-treatment. See the refusal page.

Does lesion shrinkage in two patients prove benefit?

No. Case reports generate questions; they do not establish efficacy, especially under funder-involved COI.
