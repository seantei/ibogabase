Key human studies

The direct human evidence for alcohol use disorder is nearly nonexistent: no controlled trial, and no human study of ibogaine given alone for alcohol. The only human alcohol-outcome data come from a veteran cohort that received ibogaine combined with 5-MeO-DMT and a single one-person case report. Ibogaine and noribogaine are not proven to treat alcohol use disorder. Open each source to read its full methods and limitations.

Armstrong et al., 2023Military Psychology (Mil Psychol) Prospective observational cohort (uncontrolled, single-arm; data collected during standard clinical operations at an ibogaine + 5-MeO-DMT treatment program in Mexico). Ibogaine was co-administered with 5-MeO-DMT, so ibogaine effects cannot be isolated. US Special Operations Forces Veterans; of 86 who completed treatment, 45 met criteria for risky alcohol use at pre-treatment (mean age 44; 100% male; 91% White). Cohort also had PTSD symptoms. Significant reduction in alcohol use score from pre-treatment (M=7.2, SD=2.3) to 1 month (M=3.6, SD=3.5), sustained at 6 months (M=4.0, SD=2.9; p<.001, partial eta squared=.617). At 1 month: 24% abstinent, 33% non-risky drinking, 42% risky; at 6 months: 16% abstinent, 31% non-risky drinking, 53% risky (partial relapse over time). Responders vs non-responders differed significantly on PTSD symptom and cognitive change but not demographics. 6 months PMID, journal, year, DOI, design, N, population, and all reported numbers confirmed against the PubMed abstract. Major confound: ibogaine co-administered with 5-MeO-DMT, so no isolated ibogaine effect can be attributed. Uncontrolled, no placebo/comparator, self-selected treatment-seeking veterans, all male, 91% White. Alcohol was a secondary focus alongside PTSD. Conflict of interest: author Martin Polanco affiliated with the commercial treatment program (The Mission Within). No control for regression to the mean or expectancy. Adverse events / cardiac safety not characterized in this report.
Barsuglia et al., 2018Progress in Brain Research (Prog Brain Res) Single case report (N=1) with pre/post resting-state SPECT neuroimaging. Sequential protocol: ibogaine on day 1, vaporized 5-MeO-DMT on day 3, at an inpatient clinic in Mexico; ibogaine effects not isolated. One 31-year-old male military veteran with moderate alcohol use disorder. Reported improved mood, cessation of alcohol use, and reduced cravings at 5 days post-treatment, sustained at 1 month, with a partial return to mild alcohol use at 2 months. Post-treatment SPECT showed increased perfusion in bilateral caudate nuclei, left putamen, right insula, and temporal, occipital, and cerebellar regions versus baseline. 2 months PMID, journal, year, DOI, design (case report + SPECT), N=1, and population confirmed against the PubMed abstract. N=1 anecdote; no control, no validated alcohol-use outcome instrument reported. Confounded by 5-MeO-DMT co-administration. Same clinic/program lineage as Armstrong 2023 (author Polanco; Crossroads Treatment Center / The Mission Within). Authors explicitly frame these as isolated findings requiring controlled investigation. The abstract reports weight-based doses, deliberately excluded here per the no-dosing rule. Ibogaine's documented cardiotoxicity (QT prolongation, fatal arrhythmias) was not assessed in this report.

Study details verified against PubMed. These figures describe individual studies and are not evidence of general safety or effectiveness. Ibogaine remains Schedule I in the U.S., is not an approved treatment, and carries serious, documented cardiac risks that require qualified medical supervision.
