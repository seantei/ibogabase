Culture

Culture, stewardship, and conservation

LAST REVIEWED 2026-05-18 · 272 SOURCES · 0 CORRECTIONS

Iboga begins in Central African forest and living Bwiti traditions. Public cultural context is included; restricted or lineage-held knowledge is not printed.

Public sources place Iboga in western equatorial Africa and describe older Central African knowledge systems before colonial and biomedical documentation.

Misoko, Missoko, and Fang are not interchangeable. IbogaBase treats terminology as source-specific and names which tradition a record discusses when sources allow.

Lineage-held knowledge stays with its holders. IbogaBase does not print initiation instructions, restricted songs, private ritual sequences, sacred designs, dosing instructions, or claims of authority that have not been granted.

Conservation, reciprocity, benefit-sharing, sourcing pressure, and Gabonese context are central to any ethical or commercial claim about Iboga.
