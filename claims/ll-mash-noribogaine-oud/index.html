Mash interviews: ibogaine, noribogaine, and OUD research history

LAST REVIEWED 2026-09-22 · RESEARCHER INTERVIEW · OBSERVATIONAL PRIMARY

Two high-confidence Listening Library interviews with Deborah Mash on research history, noribogaine interest, and addiction-interruption hypotheses. Podcasts are media; pair claims to Mash-authored primary literature and keep FDA-approval myths separate.

Testable claims extracted (media → primary)

  1. Claim (media): Ibogaine can transition some opioid- or cocaine-dependent people from dependence toward abstinence in observational settings.
    Primary (observational): Mash et al., “Ibogaine Detoxification Transitions Opioid and Cocaine Abusers Between Dependence and Abstinence: Clinical Observations and Treatment Outcomes,” Frontiers in Pharmacology 2018;9:529. PMID 29922156, DOI 10.3389/fphar.2018.00529. Label: clinical observations — not an RCT proving cure.
  2. Claim (media): Safety / pharmacokinetics complexity and cardiac concerns are central to clinical development.
    Primary: Mash et al., “Ibogaine: Complex Pharmacokinetics, Concerns for Safety, and Preliminary Efficacy Measures,” Ann N Y Acad Sci 2000;914:394–401. PMID 11085338, DOI 10.1111/j.1749-6632.2000.tb05213.x. Pair with current QTc desk.
  3. Claim (policy / media): Investigational noribogaine activity equals FDA approval of noribogaine or ibogaine.
    Verdict: False — see Noribogaine FDA-approval claim check.
  4. Claim (overclaim): “Ibogaine cures addiction.”
    Verdict: Overstated — cure-language check + OUD evidence.

Verdict

Useful research-history media; observational human signals exist; cure / approval language remains overstated or false. Interviews do not replace denominators, controlled trials, or cardiac-risk literacy.
