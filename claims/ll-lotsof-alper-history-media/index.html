Testable claims extracted (media → primary)

  1. Claim (historical / media): Early observational reports described acute opioid-withdrawal interruption after ibogaine exposure in uncontrolled settings.
    Primary: Alper KR, Lotsof HS, Frenken GM, Luciano DJ, Bastiaans J. “Treatment of acute opioid withdrawal with ibogaine.” Am J Addict 1999. PMID 10506904, DOI 10.1080/105504999305848. Label: historical clinical observations — not an RCT.
  2. Claim (historical): Case-management literature from the same era discusses patient-management implications without establishing modern standard care.
    Primary: Lotsof HS, Alexander NE. “Case studies of ibogaine treatment: implications for patient management strategies.” Alkaloids Chem Biol 2001. PMID 11705114.
  3. Claim (subculture documentation): A documented “ibogaine medical subculture” existed around lay / underground practice.
    Primary: Alper KR, Lotsof HS, Kaplan CD. PMID 18029124. Documentation ≠ endorsement or referral.
  4. Claim (safety update): Later channel pharmacology strengthened cardiac-risk caution around iboga alkaloids.
    Primary: Alper et al. hERG blockade PMID 25636206 → QTc desk.
  5. Claim (myth): Historical interruption stories mean FDA approval or safe DIY use.
    Verdict: False / dangerous framing — FDA check + DIY refusal.

Verdict

Historically important observational literature; still not modern confirmatory evidence, not approval, and not a how-to. Prefer evidence desks for withdrawal/OUD grades over podcast nostalgia.
