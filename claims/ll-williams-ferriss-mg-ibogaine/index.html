Williams / Ferriss: magnesium–ibogaine for TBI/PTSD

Testable claims extracted (media → primary)

  1. Claim (media): Magnesium–ibogaine regimens used in special-operations veterans are associated with large improvements on TBI/PTSD symptom measures.
    Primary check: Cherian et al., “Magnesium–ibogaine therapy in veterans with traumatic brain injuries,” Nature Medicine 2024;30:373–381. PMID 38182784, DOI 10.1038/s41591-023-02705-w. Design: open-label / observational — not a confirmatory RCT.
  2. Claim (media): Cardiac / QT risk can be “de-risked” enough that research is ethically discussable.
    IbogaBase boundary: QT/QTc remains a standing safety theme — see Safety / QTc. Podcast reassurance is not a clearance protocol and IbogaBase gives no dosing or screening recipes.
  3. Claim (press / amplification risk): Institutional or influencer language that the Stanford work “proved” TBI/PTSD treatment.
    Counter-check: Gorman DM, 2026, Int J Drug Policy — registration non-adherence atop methodological weakness. PMID 42330793. Full card: Stanford Mg-ibogaine critique.

Verdict

Media / observational — not proven cure; not FDA-approved. Use the podcast to find the paper; grade the paper by design limits. Miracle or “proved TBI/PTSD” language outruns the evidence desk.
