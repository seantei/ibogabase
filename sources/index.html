Activism history

Dana Beal

Beal is a central figure in the U.S. ibogaine underground and its decades of public advocacy for ibogaine as an addiction-interruption tool.

  • Dana Beal is a longtime political activist who, through the group Cures-not-Wars, helped organize and publicize ibogaine as a proposed treatment for opioid dependence from the 1980s onward.
  • He is frequently described in journalism and reference sources as an ibogaine activist rather than a researcher or clinician.
  • His advocacy is part of the movement history around ibogaine, separate from the peer-reviewed evidence and the safety literature.

Related searches: Dana Beal · Cures-not-Wars · ibogaine activist

What this does not prove: Activist advocacy is not clinical evidence; his role in ibogaine history says nothing about whether ibogaine is safe or effective.

Guidelines and education

Global Ibogaine Therapy Alliance (GITA)

GITA is a widely cited source of ibogaine safety and clinical-guideline material that a safety-first reference should track.

  • The Global Ibogaine Therapy Alliance (GITA) published Clinical Guidelines for Ibogaine-Assisted Treatment, a frequently cited document in ibogaine safety discussion.
  • Those clinical guidelines emphasize cardiac screening, medical monitoring, exclusion criteria, and emergency readiness.
  • IbogaBase describes GITA guidelines as source material, not as an IbogaBase-endorsed or IbogaBase-published protocol.

Related searches: GITA · Global Ibogaine Therapy Alliance · clinical guidelines

What this does not prove: Published guidelines are source material describing risk-management practice; they are not IbogaBase endorsement, not proof of efficacy, and not a protocol to follow.

Savant HWP is central to the 18-MC development history in the ibogaine-analog lane.

Conservation, sourcing, and benefit-sharing

Iboga accuracy depends on conservation and legal sourcing, not only clinical data. Public sources document wild-harvest pressure on Tabernanthe iboga in Gabon, cultivation efforts, and traceability of plant material from forest to product. Access-and-benefit-sharing under the Nagoya Protocol is the concrete legal instrument for Gabonese and community benefit, alongside conservation groups such as Blessings of the Forest. A "sustainable" or "ethical" sourcing label is a claim until traceability, current legal documentation, and benefit-sharing are shown.

Texas ibogaine funding, in primary-source terms

Texas Senate Bill 2308 (89th Legislature, 2025), authored by State Senator Tan Parker, directed a state-funded ibogaine clinical-research effort. In December 2025 the University of Texas Medical Branch (UTMB), with UTHealth Houston, was awarded $50 million by the state to lead ibogaine clinical trials. A funding appropriation and a trial program are not FDA approval, not treatment access, and not proof of effectiveness.

Mid-century and historical sources

Ibogaine's commercial and pharmacological history predates modern research. A Tabernanthe extract was sold in France as Lambarene, and the company CIBA studied ibogaine and related alkaloids in the mid-twentieth century. These are historical commercial and pharmacology records, not evidence of modern safety or effectiveness.

Wanted sources

These are the gaps that would most improve the public reference.

  • Named medical reviewers: Cardiology, addiction medicine, emergency medicine, pharmacology, and psychiatry reviewers who are willing to be listed.
  • Named cultural advisors: Gabonese, Bwiti/Bwete-linked, conservation, and stewardship advisors only when they consent to public naming.
  • Adverse-event case material: Peer-reviewed case reports, agency records, and serious journalism with enough detail to separate signal from speculation.
  • State-policy primary records: Bill text, fiscal notes, committee reports, hearing video, agency pages, and final signed law when available.
  • Primary media links: Original podcast, YouTube, documentary, book, and interview pages rather than reposted summaries.
