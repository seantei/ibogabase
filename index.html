Safety desk

Cardiac and interaction risk comes first.

Benefit claims are hard to interpret without the safety frame.

Published safety literature repeatedly centers the heart. QT/QTc prolongation, bradycardia, rhythm events, electrolyte abnormalities, medication interactions, polysubstance exposure, and emergency readiness shape how Ibogaine evidence is read.

QT/QTc
QT interval, QTc correction, bradycardia, arrhythmia concern, and electrolyte context appear repeatedly in safety literature.
CYP2D6
Metabolism and medication interactions matter when reading Ibogaine, Noribogaine, and analog claims.
Electrolytes
Withdrawal physiology, dehydration, electrolytes, cardiac risk, and polysubstance use can change safety interpretation.

Emergency readiness

Reputable research and clinical discussions emphasize screening, monitoring, exclusion criteria, medication review, and the ability to respond to cardiac or medical emergencies.

Claim checks

Common statements get a verdict.

Cure language, FDA-approval claims, clinic success rates, and “natural means safe” claims need source checks before they are repeated.

VERDICT Overstated

“Ibogaine cures addiction.”

Evidence suggests possible effects on withdrawal, craving, and relapse for some people, but cure language outruns the evidence and hides risk.

OUD evidence · Safety
VERDICT False

Trust architecture

IbogaBase is a public reference library: it separates source type, evidence strength, policy status, safety risk, culture, and claim language before drawing conclusions.

Boundaries

No gurus, no miracle claims, no borrowed authority.

IbogaBase is not legal advice or medical advice, refuses dosing, provider referral, self-treatment, and individualized medical or legal advice, and keeps support models away from referral incentives.

Legal and rules

State bills, agency records, and trial registries are legal and research signals only. They do not override federal Schedule I status unless a primary source says so.

Citation-only assistant

Ask IbogaBase answers from indexed records and shows citations; it does not answer dosing, referrals, self-treatment, or individualized medical/legal questions.

Weekly field brief

Current movement is summarized as a field brief with what changed, what it means, and what it does not mean.

Review metadata

Important changes are dated, corrections stay visible, and review status distinguishes source-compiled reference from expert endorsement.

Evidence matrix

Condition rows use the same template: evidence level, human studies, limitations, safety signals, and what is not proven.

Ethical support

Referral fees and pay-to-rank clinics are excluded. Merch, if used, stays transparent and culturally careful. No sacred symbols, initiation imagery, or Bwiti-associated designs without explicit permission.

Clinic Observatory rules

Clinic pages are monitored as claims about clinics, not as endorsements, rankings, referrals, or proof of outcomes.

Quality review

Site structure, source labels, broken links, safety-boundary drift, and confusing language are checked before publication by the Site Readability and UX Reviewer and Legal and Rules Reviewer.
