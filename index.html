Separate The Terms

Iboga, Ibogaine, Noribogaine, and analogs are not the same thing.

This distinction is central to credibility. Plant tradition, extracted alkaloid, metabolite or derivative, and biotech analogs belong in related but separate lanes.

Iboga

The plant, its Central African contexts, Gabon/Bwiti-linked stewardship, conservation, access, and cultural meaning.

Ibogaine

The psychoactive indole alkaloid studied for substance use and other conditions, with serious safety and legal constraints.

Noribogaine

A metabolite and development focus. FDA's April 2026 action allowed an early U.S. study to proceed; it was not approval or proof of safety or effectiveness.

Analogs

18-MC, Tabernanthalog, Ibogainalogs, and other derivatives are tracked by molecule, evidence type, sponsor, and clinical status.

What We Do Not Know

Serious readers trust visible uncertainty.

IbogaBase keeps uncertainty visible. Unknowns are not weaknesses; they are the map of what responsible research, policy, and journalism still need to answer.

Long-term outcomes

Durability, relapse patterns, and quality-of-life outcomes remain unclear across settings and populations.

Relapse rates

Published and clinic-reported rates vary, often without consistent definitions, follow-up windows, or denominator clarity.

Mechanisms

Pharmacology is complex and debated; plausible mechanisms are not simplified into single-cause explanations.

Screening standards

International practices vary, and marketing language often omits what screening and monitoring actually include.

Mortality denominator data

Case reports matter, but the true denominator across underground, clinic, ceremonial, and research contexts is incomplete.

Cultural authority

Outsider claims do not imply permission, initiation, lineage, or authority they do not have.

Culture, History, and Stewardship

Iboga begins in Central African forest and living Bwiti traditions.

Tabernanthe iboga grows wild in Central African rainforest, with Gabon holding a central place in public histories of the plant, Bwiti, and modern stewardship. IbogaBase treats that history as living knowledge, not as a backdrop for Ibogaine medicine.

Origins are not a single neat date

Public sources place Iboga in western equatorial Africa and describe older forest knowledge later carried into Bwiti forms among Mitsogo, Apindji, Masango, Pove, Fang, and other Gabonese communities. IbogaBase names uncertainty instead of flattening oral history into one origin myth.

Misoko, Missoko, and Fang are not interchangeable

Misoko or Missoko, sometimes written Mossoko in secondary material, is usually discussed with healing, diagnosis, and medicinal knowledge in southern Gabonese Bwiti lineages. Fang Bwiti has its own history of adoption, adaptation, ancestor veneration, and in some branches Christian contact. Each source label names which tradition the record discusses.

Lineage-held knowledge stays with its holders

IbogaBase can cover history, terminology, conservation, benefit sharing, law, and published interviews. It does not print initiation instructions, private ceremonial scripts, restricted songs, sacred designs, dosing instructions, or claims of authority that have not been granted.

Stewardship is part of the story

Conservation, poaching pressure, legal export, cultivation, Nagoya Protocol benefit-sharing, and local access belong beside every biomedical or commercial claim about Iboga. A legal supply chain is not just a business detail; it affects Gabonese communities and forests.

Claim Checks

Common statements get a verdict and supporting sources.

The goal is not to shame curiosity. The goal is to keep a powerful story from becoming medical misinformation, commercial hype, or cultural extraction.

Verdict: overstated

“Ibogaine cures addiction.”

Evidence suggests possible effects on withdrawal, craving, and relapse for some people, but cure language outruns the evidence and hides risk.

Verdict: false

“The FDA allowed Noribogaine, so it is approved.”

FDA allowed an early phase study to proceed. That is not approval and does not establish safety or effectiveness.

Verdict: false

“Natural means safe.”

Iboga and Ibogaine can be natural and still carry serious cardiac and interaction risks.

Verdict: weak evidence

“A clinic success rate proves efficacy.”

Outcome claims need denominator, follow-up window, definition of success, adverse-event reporting, conflicts, and independent review.

Safety and Legal Boundaries

Educational information, not treatment or legal guidance.

IbogaBase is not legal advice and is not medical advice. It is an educational reference that links to original sources, summarizes in its own words, labels uncertainty, and keeps commercial relationships visible.

Medical claims

No treatment promises

Condition pages separate early evidence, observational reports, mechanisms, and speculation. IbogaBase does not provide dosing, provider referrals, self-treatment instructions, or individualized medical guidance.

Regulatory status

IND is not approval

FDA-cleared study activity, including early-phase noribogaine research, is investigational. Permission for a study to proceed is not FDA approval and not proof of safety or effectiveness.
