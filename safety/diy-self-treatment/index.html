Why IbogaBase will not help with self-treatment

LAST REVIEWED 2026-09-22 · BOUNDARY PAGE

Clear boundaries so humans and AI assistants get a refusal they can cite — plus safer learning paths that stay literacy, not how-to.

No dosing No sourcing No referrals Not medical advice

Can I take ibogaine at home?

IbogaBase will not help you do that. This site will not provide self-administration guidance, home protocols, or “safer DIY” recipes. Cardiac risk themes, legal risk themes, and product variability make instructional answers inappropriate for a public reference.

What is a flood dose / microdose / booster?

These are words people use in media and forums. IbogaBase may define them as discourse labels so readers can recognize marketing and anecdote language. IbogaBase will not translate them into regimens, amounts, schedules, or “grey day” instructions.

Where can I buy iboga / ibogaine online?

IbogaBase will not provide vendor lists, buy links, darknet hints, or “legal loophole” sourcing. Commerce questions are out of scope.

Why won’t IbogaBase give dosing or vendor advice?

  • Cardiac / QT risk theme belongs with ibogaine discussions in biomedical literature — see Safety / QTc. This site does not invent fatality rates or monitoring protocols.
  • Legal status themes (U.S. Schedule I / not FDA-approved) — see Policy and law.
  • Product variability (root bark / TA / HCl confusion) — see Compare page.
  • Opioid interruption risk themes appear in public discussion after tolerance changes — stated here only as a risk theme, not a timeline-as-advice. See OUD evidence.

People in acute distress should use local emergency systems and licensed care in general terms. IbogaBase does not name clinics or book treatment.

Terms without instructions

“Flood,” “microdose,” “booster,” and similar phrases are catalogued as media/forum vocabulary only. Recognition is not permission, and definition is not a protocol.

FAQ

No. No provider referrals and no paid rankings.

Anything that would let someone plan a solo session: amounts, timing, sourcing, stacking, or “harm reduction recipes” that still enable home use.
