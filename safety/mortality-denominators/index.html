Mortality denominators — how to read case reports vs rates

LAST REVIEWED 2026-09-22 · METHODS · NO PROTOCOLS

Fatalities temporally associated with ibogaine are documented in the forensic and toxicology literature. A trustworthy population fatality rate still requires a trustworthy exposure denominator — which public data do not currently provide. This page explains that gap; it is not a protocol and not emergency guidance.

Case reports ≠ rates Incomplete denominator No dosing / no protocols

Case reports and case series vs incidence rates

Case reports / case series can establish that serious harm has occurred and describe setting (unsupervised use, polysubstance exposure, pre-existing cardiac disease, lack of monitoring). They cannot, by themselves, yield “1 in X” risk statements.

A rate needs both a numerator (events meeting a definition) and a denominator (total exposures or person-time in a defined population). For ibogaine, most use occurs outside registries, trials, and standardized clinics, so the global exposure denominator is incomplete. Statements like “the death rate is one in X” are not supportable from the current public record. See also the Safety desk section “Why fatality rates cannot be calculated.”

What existing safety literature patterns show (source-labeled)

  • Alper et al., 2012 — systematic review of fatalities temporally associated with ibogaine outside West Central Africa (1990–2008); 19 deaths within 1.5–76 hours of ingestion in the series. PMID 22268458, DOI 10.1111/j.1556-4029.2011.02008.x. Establishes harm and context clustering — not a population rate.
  • Litjens & Brunt, 2016 — toxicology review (“How toxic is ibogaine?”). PMID 26807959, DOI 10.3109/15563650.2016.1138226.
  • Cardiac mechanism theme — human iPSC-cardiomyocyte work shows action-potential prolongation at relevant concentrations (e.g., PMID 27020671). Mechanism literacy ≠ dosing advice.
  • Monitored QTc observation — Knuijver et al., Addiction 2022, documents sizable QTc prolongation under monitoring in a small inpatient opioid-dependent cohort (PMID 33620733). Safety physiology under monitoring is not a fatality rate.

How to read headlines after a celebrity or clinic death

  1. Separate temporal association from proven causality.
  2. Ask whether toxicology, ECG history, concomitant substances, and setting are actually reported.
  3. Refuse “one in X” math when the denominator is unknown.
  4. Keep QT / cardiac literacy in view — Safety / QTc.
  5. Do not treat entertainment media as forensic adjudication — see TMZ observatory card.

What this page refuses

No dosing, no self-screening checklists, no emergency protocols, no clinic ranking or referral. Educational methods literacy only.
