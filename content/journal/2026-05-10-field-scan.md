# May 2026 Field Review: Why IbogaBase Needs a Source Desk

Reviewed: 2026-05-10

Iboga and Ibogaine are no longer niche topics moving in one lane. The field is moving across federal policy, state funding, veteran-focused research, company strategy, Noribogaine development, conservation, and cultural stewardship at the same time.

## What Changed

- FDA announced that an early-phase U.S. Noribogaine hydrochloride study for alcohol use disorder may proceed.
- Texas moved from policy authorization to a publicly funded Ibogaine clinical-trial consortium.
- Nature-family publications continue to analyze the Stanford-linked veteran TBI cohort.
- Company press releases are increasing around Ibogaine-related intellectual property and federal psychedelic policy.
- ICEERS and conservation organizations continue to emphasize that Iboga is a cultural and ecological subject, not only a molecule.

## Editorial Position

IbogaBase should track every public item, but not treat every item as equal.

Primary documents, laws, trial registries, and peer-reviewed papers should sit at the center. Company announcements, clinic marketing, videos, and X posts should be logged and checked, but clearly labeled as claims until independently supported.

## What IbogaBase Should Do Next

- Build a living source desk.
- Publish a monthly "what changed" report.
- Create separate trackers for trials, laws, safety issues, clinics, cultural stewardship, and media claims.
- Require evidence labels on every article.
- Keep cultural review and medical review separate, visible, and respected.
